# Phase 1 — Customer pain-point map

Format per pain: what the customer says · evidence · severity · who already solves it ·
solved / partially solved / **underserved** · what it implies for us.

Severity is 1–5 from the *buyer's* point of view (does it cost them money or credibility this
quarter), not ours.

---

## P1. "I have no idea whether AI recommends us"

- **Voice of customer:** "We also recently added Waikay for brand monitoring across ChatGPT,
  Perplexity, Gemini and Claude… it shows frequency, the context you appear in, and even where the
  model's description doesn't match reality" (r/SEO). Also: "we need a reliable metric to track it
  first" before investing in GEO (r/SEO).
- **Evidence:** B3/B4 (51% of B2B software buyers start in AI chat; chatbots are the #1 shortlist
  influence at 54%); M3 (Search Console will never measure ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude); M1 (GSC's new
  AI report gives impressions only — no clicks, no queries).
- **Severity:** 4.
- **Already solved by:** Peec ($95–495/mo), Otterly ($29–489), Scrunch ($300–500), Semrush AI
  Visibility ($99/domain), Ahrefs Brand Radar, HubSpot AEO ($45–50/mo + free Grader), Profound
  (enterprise), plus ~30 tools averaging $337/mo (F6, F2).
- **Status: SOLVED — and commoditizing.** Google now gives AI-impression data free (M1); HubSpot
  gives a grader free; Semrush and Ahrefs bundle it into suites buyers already pay for; two of the
  best independent vendors were acquired in 12 months (F4, F5); the category leader withdrew
  self-serve pricing to go enterprise-only (F2).
- **Implication:** **Do not build a monitoring dashboard as the product.** It is the entry fee, not
  the offer. We must be able to produce a number, but we get paid for what happens after it.

## P2. "Our organic traffic is falling and I can't tell why or what to do"

- **Evidence:** E1 (organic CTR 0.61% with AI Overviews vs 1.62% without; uncited brands' CTR fell
  67% over 2025), E2 (position-1 CTR −58%), E3 (8% click rate with an AI Overview vs 15% without —
  Pew, independent), E5 (−38% organic clicks in a randomized field experiment, [UNVERIFIED] primary),
  E6 (publisher referrals −33%), P12 (Indian sites ~−22% on AI-Overview queries).
- **Severity:** 5 for content-dependent businesses; 3 for those whose demand is brand-led.
- **Already solved by:** every SEO agency and tool on earth — for *diagnosis*.
- **Status: PARTIALLY SOLVED.** Diagnosis is abundant; *remediation that is known to work* is not.
  Note E1's stabilization signal (AIO-query CTR 1.3% → 2.4% between Dec 2025 and Feb 2026): the
  trend is not monotone, and a vendor selling panic will be caught out within two quarters.
- **Implication:** Sell the remedy and the evidence grade, not the diagnosis. And publish the
  stabilization data ourselves — it is against interest, which makes it credible.

## P3. "I know what I should do and I have nobody to do it"

- **Evidence:** P7 (**92% of marketers plan to optimize for AI search; only 40.6% are doing so** —
  vendor-generated, directional); P4 (GEO retainers $1,500–$10,000/mo; mid-market $3,000–$12,000;
  enterprise $10,000–$50,000+); P6 (94% of enterprise CMOs plan to increase AEO/GEO spend);
  H1/H2 (companies are hiring $65k–$318k roles, i.e. internalizing — which is precisely what a
  $5M company cannot do); P2 (56.2% of SEO agencies raised prices citing AI-search work).
- **Severity:** 5.
- **Already solved by (v2, corrected 16 Oct 2026):**
  *In software:* Peec Actions + content optimizer + Peec Agent ("owned actions on your own pages and
  earned actions such as getting listed, pitching editorial, and growing reviews"; "Nothing changes
  without your approval"), Profound AI Marketer/Agents (drafts work, stages it, writes into WordPress,
  Sanity, Contentful and Framer), Scrunch/Sitecore (AEO opportunities with projected citation lift,
  apply-or-decline in the CMS Page Builder) — at €70–€360/mo, $399/mo per agency workspace and enterprise
  custom pricing respectively. *In services:* GEO agencies from **$997/mo** (SourceRank: "GEO and AEO done
  for them, measurement included"), **$2,000–$5,000/mo** ("tool plus contractor"), **$2,500/mo per pillar
  or $5,500/mo fully managed** (MADX), up to $20,000–$30,000/mo, plus white-label digital-PR suppliers who
  will let any agency resell the workstream. *In-house:* $127k–$318k roles.
- **Status (v2): SOLVED IN SOFTWARE AT €70–€360/MONTH, AND SOLD AS A SERVICE FROM $997/MONTH.** v1's
  "almost nothing between a $99 dashboard and a $5,000 agency" was wrong on both ends: the dashboard now
  drafts and stages the fix, and the service tier now starts at $997. What is left is narrower — see
  implication.
- **Implication (v2):** the remaining gap is **not** capability or price band. It is *who does the work and
  who is accountable for it*: software leaves execution to the client's team ("It identifies the
  opportunity; your team still executes it" — third-party review of Peec, Aug 2026), and agencies that do
  execute mostly serve US/EU brands or Indian domestic clients. Our wedge is therefore a **[HYPOTHESIS]**,
  not a finding: *managed human delivery for India-origin software companies selling to US/UK/EU, at the
  bottom of the published service band, with disclosed measurement uncertainty.* Stage 1's buy-vs-hire card
  (`08` §7.1) exists to test exactly this, and ≥4 of 5 buyers preferring the tool kills it.
  Peec's own review summary says it best about a competitor: "No content generation, no changes to
  your site. This is the single most important line on the page" (F6).

## P4. "The changes that would actually move the needle are on websites I don't own"

- **Evidence:** M8 (**85% of commercial brand discovery came from third-party sources, not owned
  domains**; brands are **6.5x** more likely to be cited via third-party sources than via their own
  domain; ~90% of third-party mentions come from listicles, comparison pages and review roundups;
  ~80% of mentioned brands appear in the first three positions of that third-party page);
  C1 (top-cited domains: Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, Forbes, **G2**, Yelp, Medium);
  C2 (YouTube = 20.9% of AI Overview citations); C3 (Reddit ≈46.7% of Perplexity citations);
  C4 (top 15 domains ≈68% of citation share; only ~11% of cited domains appear on more than one
  platform); O1 (adding citations/quotes/statistics raises visibility, but the biggest gains went to
  low-ranked sources: rank-5 pages +115.1%).
- **Severity:** 5 — and it is the pain customers do not yet have language for.
- **Already solved by:** digital-PR agencies, manually, expensively, and without measurement.
  Scrunch's AXP is the only productized attempt we found, and it addresses *serving machine-readable
  versions of your own pages*, i.e. the owned-domain side (F4).
- **Status (v2, corrected): the *recommendation* layer is occupied; the *measured, ethical delivery*
  layer is contested.** Peec already names earned actions (getting listed, pitching editorial, growing
  reviews) and surfaces "third-party channels worth working with" across YouTube/Reddit/LinkedIn/TikTok;
  Profound sells "opportunities to secure earned media placements at highly cited sources" and drafts
  publisher corrections; Scrunch publishes prioritised recommendations with projected citation lift;
  digital-PR-for-GEO retainers run $3,000–$10,000/mo mid-market with white-label suppliers behind them.
  Two hard constraints from the evidence: **95% of AI-cited links come from non-paid sources** (Muck Rack
  analysis of 1M+ citations, cited by an agency playbook — not independently verified), so placement cannot
  be bought; and a syndication tactic claiming a **median 239% citation lift** (Stacker + Scrunch, Mar 2026,
  unverified) is the spam-adjacent shortcut competitors will sell and we will not. What remains open is
  **[HYPOTHESIS]**: measured, ethical, segment-specialised earned-media delivery at mid-market price.
- **Implication:** This is our differentiation thesis #2 (see `09`). It is also our biggest ethical
  and execution risk: the moment "get cited on third-party pages" becomes "manufacture third-party
  endorsement", we violate our own integrity rules. The workflow must be: identify the citation
  gateway → correct/complete factual records we are entitled to control (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius,
  Gartner Peer Insights, Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Wikipedia where notability and sourcing genuinely
  permit) → pitch existing listicle/comparison authors with verified facts and assets → produce
  genuinely citable owned assets (data, benchmarks, transcripts, comparison pages). No paid placement
  disguised as editorial, no astroturfed community content, no fake reviews.
- **Hard truth to state in sales:** this workstream is slow (weeks to months), partly outside our
  control, and cannot be guaranteed. If a client needs a guarantee, they are not our client.

## P5. "I cannot prove ROI to my CFO"

- **Evidence:** E7 (AI referrals = 1.08% of traffic but ~2x conversion and ~1/3 the sessions to
  close — Conductor); E8 (AI = ~0.24% of global internet traffic); E10 (conversion premiums of
  4.4x–23x, mostly single-property or vendor-reported); **E11 (contradiction: a 973-site e-commerce
  study found organic beat ChatGPT referrals by ~13% for transactional purchases)**; M3 (no
  first-party prompt-level attribution exists); P7 (GEO ROI is structurally harder to measure than
  SEO because zero-click answers generate no referral session and shortlist influence happens before
  any trackable touchpoint); M14 (practitioners openly call the category snake oil for exactly this
  reason).
- **Severity:** 5 for whoever signs the cheque; 3 for whoever feels the pain.
- **Already solved by:** nobody. Vendors *claim* it (O4: Ramp 7x visibility, CRS +15% pipeline,
  Rough Country $22,700 attributed revenue) but every one of those is vendor- or agency-reported.
- **Status: UNSOLVED, and probably unsolvable in the strong form.** No one can prove incremental
  revenue from AI visibility with a clean counterfactual at mid-market scale.
- **Implication:** We must not sell causation. We sell a **three-tier evidence stack**:
  (T-a) leading indicators — mentions, citations, share of answer, with confidence intervals and
  run counts; (T-b) acquisition indicators — GA4 AI-referral sessions, conversions and pipeline
  tagged by source, plus Search Console AI-impression trends (M1); (T-c) commercial indicators —
  CRM self-attribution ("how did you hear about us" / "what led you to evaluate us"), demo-request
  notes, and closed-won deals where the buyer names an AI answer. Tier C is *reported*, never
  *claimed as caused*. Clients can verify B and C without us — which is the only reason a CFO will
  believe anything.

## P6. "AI says something false or outdated about us"

- **Evidence:** practitioner reports of tools that surface "where the model's description doesn't
  match reality" (M14, r/SEO); O4 (Scrunch/Sitecore positioning around brands being "missing,
  misrepresented, or cited less often"); C6 (AI search surfaces more low-credibility sources than
  traditional search); O1 (models reward verifiable evidence texture).
- **Severity:** 5 when it happens on a commercial query (wrong pricing, wrong compliance posture,
  wrong integration list — a security vendor being described as lacking SOC 2 loses the deal);
  2 when it's cosmetic.
- **Already solved by:** partially, as a *detection* feature in several tools. Remediation
  (getting the underlying third-party record fixed) is manual and unsolved.
- **Status: PARTIALLY SOLVED / UNDERSERVED on remediation.**
- **Implication:** An excellent, concrete, emotionally resonant sales artefact: "here are the three
  false statements ChatGPT and Perplexity make about your product, here is the source page each one
  came from, here is the fix and who owns it." High close rate potential, low delivery cost, and it
  is *verifiable by the client in 60 seconds* — which satisfies integrity rule 23.

## P7. "AI crawlers can't read our site"

- **Evidence:** A8 (allowing GPTBot/ClaudeBot/PerplexityBot in robots.txt listed as "critical" for
  exporters); M2 (Google's opt-out toggle is all-or-nothing per site); F6 (Ahrefs Brand Radar gives
  AI-crawler traffic free); C5 (only ~15% of pages ChatGPT retrieves appear in the final answer;
  44% of ChatGPT citations come from the first third of content).
- **Severity:** 3 (necessary condition, rarely sufficient).
- **Status: SOLVED, and given away free.**
- **Implication:** Table stakes in an audit. Never a standalone product. It is the cheapest way to
  make a diagnostic look thorough, and the fastest way to look like a fraud if it is all we offer.

## P8. "We sell internationally from India and we are invisible where our buyers are"

- **Evidence:** A5 (55% of Indian cybersecurity product companies expanded globally but only 17%
  have overseas offices); A7 (Amazon: $20B cumulative Indian e-commerce exports, targeting $80B by
  2030; Indian digital-services exports ~$246B per Nasscom A1); A8 (hreflang is the single most
  common technical error on exporter sites; machine-translated content is de-ranked; low-difficulty
  emerging queries exist such as "how to appear in ChatGPT as supplier"); B3/B5 (the buyers doing
  AI-first research are overwhelmingly US/EU; ChatGPT holds 62% of B2B buyer chatbot usage);
  P9 (Indian agencies already sell GEO retainers at ₹20,000–₹2,80,000/mo, which is real WTP
  evidence); P3 (global mid-market B2B SaaS SEO retainers $5,000–$10,000/mo).
- **Severity:** 4 — and structurally under-priced relative to the value, because the buyer compares
  us to Indian agency rates rather than to the US revenue at stake.
- **Already solved by:** Indian SEO agencies at ₹20,000–₹1,20,000/mo, mostly without AI-answer
  measurement, mostly on-page only (i.e. aimed at the 15% lever, not the 85% lever in P4).
- **Status: UNDERSERVED.** This is the intersection where our beachhead lives.
- **Implication:** The positioning is not "GEO for Indian companies." It is: **"Your buyers in the
  US and Europe now start with an AI answer. We make sure that answer contains you — and we show
  you the evidence, in a report you can verify yourself."** Priced in USD where the buying entity
  permits (§3 of `10-tam-bottom-up.md`).

## P9. "My tool vendor's numbers don't match what I see when I ask ChatGPT myself"

- **Evidence:** M7 (Otterly's own stability study; 82% of Perplexity answers contained no brand
  mention), M9 (source sets overlap only 34–42% day to day), M10 (~95% consistency within minutes),
  M11 (re-running accounts for ~34.8% of variance; a single response carries almost no
  brand-discriminating signal), M12 (ChatGPT exact-repeat citation rate 0.06%), M13 (mention
  detection accuracy across 12 platforms averaged 81%, range 67–94%), M4 (OpenAI ToS prohibit
  programmatic extraction from the consumer product), F2 (Profound withdrew self-serve pricing and
  reviewers still quote dead plans — evidence of how fast ground truth shifts).
- **Severity:** 4 — this is the objection that kills deals in this category, and it is *legitimate*.
- **Already solved by:** nobody. Every vendor has an incentive to present a stable score.
- **Status: UNSOLVED and structurally uncomfortable for incumbents.**
- **Implication:** **Radical measurement honesty is a differentiator we can actually ship.**
  Every report carries run counts, time windows, confidence intervals, per-surface separation
  (never one blended "AI visibility score"), the disclosure that API surfaces ≠ consumer
  experience, and an invitation for the client to re-run any prompt themselves. This costs us
  nothing and directly attacks the "snake oil" objection (M14) that incumbents cannot answer
  without undermining their own dashboards.
