# Three-option comparison card + buyer investigation set

**Purpose:** replace v1's two-option buy-vs-hire card with the three commercially realistic options the founder
specified (A4), each with comparable deliverables, and to standardise what we ask every buyer.
**Gate:** wording requires approval **D3** (`01-founder-decision-checklist.md`) before it is shown to anyone.
**Rules:** present all three without recommending one; read Option C's price as *under test*, not as a quote;
record answers verbatim; log counts, not percentages (n = 5).

Prices below are what we will say out loud. Each is sourced in `02-evidence-register.md` §13–§14 and
`09-competitive-threats.md` §4. Do not state a competitor price that has not been re-verified within 30 days.

---

## The card (read verbatim, then hand over)

> You have three realistic ways to handle AI-search visibility. They differ mainly in **who does the work**
> and **who is accountable for it**, not in what information is available.
>
> **Option A — Buy the software, execute in-house.**
> A platform such as Peec ($95–$495/month), Otterly ($29–$489) or Semrush's AI Visibility toolkit
> ($99/domain) tracks your mentions and citations across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI surfaces,
> ranks the opportunities — split into *owned* actions on your pages and *earned* actions such as getting
> listed, pitching editorial and growing reviews — drafts content changes you can accept, edit or reject
> individually, and connects to Google Analytics and Search Console so you can see AI referrals, conversions
> and revenue. Nothing changes without your approval.
> **Your team does the work: roughly 6–10 hours a month**, and someone still has to pitch the editors, update
> the review-site profiles and produce the assets.
>
> **Option B — Hire an agency or freelancer.**
> Published GEO/AEO retainers run from about **$997/month** for a done-for-you service with measurement
> included, **$2,000–$5,000/month** for a tool-plus-contractor arrangement, **$2,500/month per pillar or
> $5,500/month fully managed**, and **$3,000–$10,000/month** for digital-PR-led programmes, up to
> $20,000–$30,000/month at enterprise. Indian agencies publish ₹19,999–₹2,80,000/month. They do the work;
> **your internal effort is typically 2–5 hours a month** for briefing and approvals. Quality and disclosure
> vary widely, and most reporting comes from the same dashboards available in Option A.
>
> **Option C — SignalGEO managed execution.**
> We do the measurement to a written protocol — fixed prompt sets, five runs per prompt, two windows at least
> seven days apart, results reported **per surface with confidence intervals**, never blended into one score —
> and we do the work: on-site optimisation staged for your approval with item-by-item diffs and rollback, and
> off-site corroboration, meaning we pitch the listicles, comparison pages and editors that AI actually cites,
> correct and complete the profiles we are entitled to control, and produce the assets that get cited. Every
> claim we publish is traced to a business fact **you** have verified. We never promise placement or ranking,
> and we publish the criteria for when you should downgrade or cancel.
> **Roughly $1,500/month (₹1,00,000)** for managed plus earned media, or **₹75,000/month** for on-site only.
> **Your internal effort: 1–2 hours a month.** That price is what we are testing — tell us if it is wrong.

## What we record for every buyer (A4 investigation set)

| # | Question | Recorded as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who owns this budget?** Name, role, and who else approves | text + is-this-the-economic-buyer Y/N |
| 2 | **What software do you already use** for AI visibility, SEO or analytics — and what do you pay? | tool names + amounts |
| 3 | **What agencies or freelancers** do you use, for what, at what monthly cost, and since when? | names + amounts + tenure |
| 4 | **Total current monthly spend** on SEO / content / PR / AI-visibility | number + currency + which entity pays |
| 5 | **What work is identified but not done?** Name the last three things you knew you should do and did not, and why | verbatim; this is the capacity evidence |
| 6 | **Internal execution capacity:** how many hours a month, and whose hours? What does an hour of that person cost? | hours + role + loaded cost estimate |
| 7 | **What specific result would justify payment?** Not "more visibility" — the deal, pipeline, region or product it has to move | verbatim |
| 8 | **What would make you switch vendors** (tool or agency) today? | verbatim |
| 9 | **Which of A / B / C would you buy — and why?** | choice + verbatim reason |
| 10 | **Price ladder:** first rung they refuse (₹40,000 → ₹75,000 → ₹1,00,000/$1,500 → $3,000); and the rung they call "too cheap to trust" | two rungs |
| 11 | **Would you sign a paid pilot, or pay a deposit?** If not, what specifically would need to change? | Y/N + condition |
| 12 | **Would you authorise** GA4 + Search Console read access and a "what led you to evaluate us?" field? Who would say no? | Y/N + blocker |
| 13 | **Co-observation:** will you run 3 runs of this exact prompt, in this window, logged out, and compare with our interval? | Y/N + result vs interval |
| 14 | Which entity would pay — **Indian (INR) or US/UK (USD)**? Does that change the number? | INR / USD / unknown |
| 15 | One thing they said that surprised us; one reason this deal will **not** happen | verbatim |

## Scoring and interpretation

### Commercial evidence tier (A5)

| Tier | Evidence | Counts toward |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Signed paid pilot, or advance payment / deposit received | Decisive — one T1 beats five T4s |
| **T2** | Formal proposal requested **with a price** + written budget confirmation, or a named procurement process with a date | Strong |
| **T3** | Decision-meeting commitment, or introduction to the economic buyer | Moderate |
| **T4** | "GEO is important", "interesting", "keep me posted", "send me more info" | **Zero — logged separately** |

### Option-choice scoring (no automatic kill)

Record: choice (A/B/C/neither/combination), verbatim reason, and the reason **category** —
`no-capacity` · `no-budget` · `prefer-control` · `already-have-tool` · `do-not-believe-measurement` ·
`trust/brand` · `price` · `timing` · `other`.

A pattern becomes a conclusion only when it is **consistent across segments and the reasons are the same**.
Examples of correct reading at n = 5:
- 3 choose A, all three citing `already-have-tool` **and** `no-capacity` is false (they have staff hours) →
  our offer is mis-positioned; test again with a sharper capacity question. **Modify, do not stop.**
- 3 choose A, all three citing `no-budget` at $1,500 but interest at ₹40,000 → price/domicile problem, not a
  demand problem. **Modify: re-test the ladder with USD-billing entities.**
- 4 choose C but zero T1/T2 evidence → **interest, not willingness to pay.** Treat as T4-equivalent; the test
  has not been passed.
- 1 signs a pilot at ₹75,000 while 3 prefer A → **the single T1 outweighs the vote.** Proceed with that client
  and keep testing.

Any signal that would have triggered a company-wide kill in v1 instead produces: (a) a written reasoning note
from the founder, (b) one named next test, (c) an entry in the deviation log. **Small samples are interpreted,
not automated.**

### Segmentation check

Because the five candidates span **3 × S1, 1 × S2, 1 × S4**, record the option choice and first-refused rung
per segment. If S2 (cybersecurity) or S4 (US/EU mid-market) responds materially better than S1, the beachhead
changes — `05-icp-scoring-and-ranking.md` §6.1 shows the model can no longer discriminate between them
(S1 3.70 = S2 3.70 > S4 3.62), so buyer responses decide.

## Language we never use

"Guaranteed", "we'll get you into ChatGPT", "rank #1 in AI", "placement secured", "proven revenue lift",
"our proprietary data shows". Also never: a competitor capability claim without a retrieval date, or a
statistic we cannot trace to a register entry. Integrity rules 7 and 8.
