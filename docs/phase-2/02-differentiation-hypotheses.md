# Stage 2 — B3: Differentiation hypotheses tested

**Workstream B3 of the founder's decision of 16 Oct 2026.** Desk research only; no spend, no contact, no code.
Prepared 16 October 2026. Evidence base: `../phase-1/02-evidence-register.md` §13–§17 and
`01-competitor-capability-matrix.md` tranches 1–2.

**Scoring:** 1 (worst) – 5 (best) per criterion, with the evidence label attached. Scores are our judgement on
documented evidence, not measurements; anything resting on buyer behaviour is a **[HYPOTHESIS]** until Stage 1
produces observations. Every hypothesis ends with the evidence that would **invalidate** it, stated before we
look for the evidence that supports it.

## H1 — Managed execution specialisation

> *SignalGEO delivers the actual work for companies without enough internal SEO/GEO capacity.*

| Criterion | Score | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pain & urgency | **4** | Documented, not inferred: 92% of marketers plan to optimise for AI search while 40.6% are doing so (vendor-generated, directional); employers publish $127k–$318k roles they cannot always fill; agencies state plainly that "**the retainer buys capacity**: you are paying for contributor time, publishing volume, and a measurement loop that a two-person marketing team cannot staff" (register §14). Urgency is real but episodic — AI referrals are still ~1% of traffic |
| Willingness to pay | **3** | Published offers bracket us: $497 one-time audit, **$997/mo done-for-you with measurement**, $2,000–$5,000/mo tool-plus-contractor, $2,500/pillar or $5,500 managed, $3,000–$10,000/mo digital-PR-for-GEO. So $1,000–$1,500/mo is **inside** an existing band, not below the market. **[NOT YET VERIFIED]** for India-origin buyers specifically |
| Competitive overlap | **2** | High. Software overlaps on everything except doing the work (matrix rows 1–11, 13–15 all ✔ for Peec/Profound/Scrunch); agencies overlap on the work itself from $997/mo, with **white-label digital-PR suppliers** removing any scarcity of pitching capacity |
| Ease of replication | **2** | Trivially replicable: it is labour plus a licensed tool. Any agency can add it in a quarter; any freelancer can undercut it |
| Service delivery complexity | **2** (i.e. complex — a cost, not a benefit) | 21 h/client/month at Tier C; earned media depends on third parties who owe us nothing; **95% of AI-cited links are non-paid**, so placement cannot be bought; deliverables must survive fact verification |
| Recurring value | **4** | Strong and evidenced: 0.06% exact-repeat citation rate, 34–42% day-to-day source overlap, ~30% run-to-run brand persistence, content >3 months stale loses >3× citations, monthly model churn. The work genuinely recurs — see B6 |
| Gross margin potential | **4** | 82% at $1,500 Tier C, 75% at ₹1,00,000 (`12` §4.2); falls to −6% at ₹40,000 with $400 tooling — margin is a function of price and tooling choice, not of effort |
| Time to first revenue | **5** | Fastest of the four: a diagnostic can be delivered in days and a pilot signed inside 30 days with **no build** |
| Distribution advantage | **2** | None inherent. Founder network and India-origin communities are a wedge, not a channel; no marketplace, no partner ecosystem |
| Scalability & defensibility | **2** | Capacity-capped: **4.3–6.2 clients per delivery FTE** at Tier C; $2M ARR requires 12–21 FTEs. Defensibility is reputation and segment trust only |
| **Weighted read** | **Viable, unremarkable, fast** | The only hypothesis with a documented path to first revenue inside 30 days and no capital. Also the one with the lowest ceiling |

**Evidence that would invalidate H1:**
1. ≥3 of 5 buyers choose Option A (software + internal execution) **and** give the reason `no-capacity` as
   false — i.e. they have spare internal hours. Then the premise ("lack sufficient execution capacity") is wrong
   for this segment.
2. A buyer names an existing vendor at ≤$997/mo that already does the work for them, with satisfaction.
3. Measured delivery hours exceed 21 h/client/month at Tier C, pushing gross margin below 60% at $1,500.
4. Peec, Profound or Scrunch ships a **managed human service** tier (not software). Their agent roadmaps make
   this a live risk within two quarters: Peec already ships an agent with 30 skills and 20+ MCP connectors, and
   Profound's agents already write into four CMSs.
5. Prospects refuse to authorise off-site work on their behalf (reputational risk of being "pitched" by a third
   party), which removes the highest-value half of the retainer.

## H2 — Vertical specialisation (cybersecurity and technically complex B2B software)

> *SignalGEO performs better for cybersecurity and technically complex B2B software because of domain-specific
> buyer questions, entity understanding and competitive intelligence.*

| Criterion | Score | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pain & urgency | **5** | Highest severity of any segment studied: security buyers research heavily in AI; a **misrepresented compliance posture is a sales-blocker**; 400+ Indian cybersecurity product firms, $4.46B (2025) → ~$6B (2026), **55% have expanded globally but only 17% maintain overseas offices** — globally ambitious, locally thin on marketing presence |
| Willingness to pay | **3** | Cybersecurity pays premium prices for specialist services generally, and Peec's own material lists a "cybersecurity PR" specialism among competitors' claims (Crackle PR). **[NOT YET VERIFIED]** for AI-search work specifically in India |
| Competitive overlap | **4** | **Best of the four.** No funded platform specialises in Indian cybersecurity product vendors; horizontal tools serve them but do not know their buyer questions, compliance vocabulary or analyst-relations dynamics |
| Ease of replication | **3** | Domain knowledge takes time to acquire but is not proprietary; an incumbent could hire it. Our advantage is being there first with references, not owning anything |
| Service delivery complexity | **3** | Higher stakes, lower volume: every claim needs verification against certification/compliance facts; a false claim about a security product is the existential ethical risk in the whole business (integrity rules 9, 24) |
| Recurring value | **4** | Threat landscape, CVEs, certifications, analyst cycles and product releases churn continuously — arguably more than horizontal SaaS |
| Gross margin potential | **4** | Same COGS structure as H1 with a price premium available for specialisation; unproven premium **[HYPOTHESIS]** |
| Time to first revenue | **3** | Slower than H1: the universe is ~130 reachable accounts, sales cycles in security are longer, and trust requires references |
| Distribution advantage | **4** | Real and specific: DSCI and Nasscom networks, security conferences, a nameable list of ~400 firms. Nobody else is working this list for AI search |
| Scalability & defensibility | **3** | Small universe caps revenue (~130 accounts ⇒ at 20% penetration and $1,500/mo, ≈$470k ARR ceiling for the vertical alone), but reputation in a tight community is genuinely hard to copy |
| **Weighted read** | **Best wedge, too small to be the company** | Use as the reference-generating second vertical, exactly as `05` §6 concluded, unless Stage 1 shows security buyers convert faster and pay more than S1 |

**Evidence that would invalidate H2:**
1. The single S2 diagnostic candidate cannot name a deal, region or analyst interaction affected by AI answers
   (severity is theoretical, not felt).
2. Security buyers refuse to let an external party touch claims about certifications or compliance — which
   removes the highest-value work and leaves generic content.
3. The reachable universe proves smaller than ~130, or the named buyers turn out to be agencies-of-record
   already, making access as hard as S4.
4. Stage 1 shows S1 or S4 converting at equal or better rates with less delivery risk → specialisation buys
   nothing and adds cost.

## H3 — Commercial-outcome intelligence

> *SignalGEO offers more useful reporting by combining AI visibility evidence with authorised first-party
> marketing and sales data, without claiming unsupported causality.*

| Criterion | Score | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pain & urgency | **4** | CFO-facing question "did it work?" is real and unanswered; agencies report that what they show the CFO is weak; GSC's generative-AI report gives impressions but **no clicks or queries** |
| Willingness to pay | **2** | Buyers pay for outcomes, but attribution here is structurally unprovable at mid-market scale; AI referrals are ~1.08% of traffic, so any revenue effect is small and noisy. Paying for an honest "we cannot attribute this" is a hard sell |
| Competitive overlap | **1** (worst) | **Occupied.** Peec connects Google Analytics and reports AI-referral sessions, engaged sessions, **conversions and revenue**, plus "visibility lift analysis" and a "visibility lift predictor"; GSC gives AI-feature impressions free; Conductor reports ~2× conversion; Profound has Agent Analytics |
| Ease of replication | **1** | It is an integration plus a report template. Peec already ships the connectors and Looker Studio / Power BI dashboards |
| Service delivery complexity | **3** | Requires client authorisation for GA4/GSC/CRM access, a self-attribution field, and careful non-causal language on every deliverable |
| Recurring value | **3** | Reporting recurs, but it is the least defensible part of a retainer — and the free GSC report sets a ceiling on what a paid version can claim to add |
| Gross margin potential | **3** | Low marginal cost, but it cannot be priced alone; it only has value inside H1 |
| Time to first revenue | **2** | Cannot be sold standalone; needs an existing client relationship and data access |
| Distribution advantage | **1** | None |
| Scalability & defensibility | **1** | None. This is a feature, and incumbents have it |
| **Weighted read** | **Do not sell as a product** | Keep as the **reporting discipline inside H1**: three evidence tiers, non-causal language, refusal to claim incremental revenue. Our differentiator is the *honesty* of the report, not the join |

**Evidence that would invalidate H3:** *(already largely invalidated)*
1. **Confirmed:** Peec reports conversions and revenue from AI referrals via GA4 and ships lift analysis and a
   lift predictor [VERIFIED, official pricing/changelog].
2. **Confirmed:** no vendor publishes intervals in output, but a vendor "lift predictor" is exactly the kind of
   unqualified causal claim our protocol forbids — so the asymmetry is ethical, not technical.
3. Would-be support: if ≥3 of 5 buyers say the thing their current tool cannot answer is "what did this do to
   pipeline", H3 gains value **as a service differentiator** even though it is not a product.

## H4 — Agency infrastructure (white-label)

> *SignalGEO provides a white-label operational workflow that helps traditional SEO agencies offer AI-search
> optimisation without building their own technology or delivery team.*

| Criterion | Score | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pain & urgency | **3** | Real for agencies without delivery capacity — but the market already solves it: **white-label digital-PR-for-GEO suppliers** let an agency resell the earned-media workstream under its own brand, and vendors sell agency tiers |
| Willingness to pay | **3** | Anchored by vendor agency pricing: Peec agency from **€205/mo** (Essential, 10,000 credits) with **free 7-day pitch projects outside the client quota**, consolidated billing and free MCP; Profound Agency Growth **$99/mo with 10 pitch workspaces** then **$399 per real client workspace**; Scrunch has a dedicated agencies section. An agency comparing us to €205 or $99 needs a very different offer |
| Competitive overlap | **2** | High on tooling (vendors own it) and high on delivery (white-label PR suppliers own it). The unoccupied slice is **measurement QA and verification** — nobody sells "we will validate your agency's AI numbers" |
| Ease of replication | **2** | Vendors can add it in a release (Peec already has agency plans, pitch projects, client seats, consolidated billing and per-project RBAC) |
| Service delivery complexity | **3** | Lower per client than direct (agency owns communication and reporting): **3–6 h/client/month**, 18–37 clients per FTE |
| Recurring value | **4** | Agencies retain clients for years; but **concentration risk is severe** — one agency with 8 clients is $2,800/mo of revenue that leaves in a single decision |
| Gross margin potential | **4** | 77–84% at $350/mo with $30 tooling; drops to 57–64% at $100 tooling (`00-integrated-plan.md` §7.3) |
| Time to first revenue | **3** | Agency sales cycles are longer than a founder's direct sale, but one deal brings many clients |
| Distribution advantage | **4** | **The only hypothesis with real leverage**: an agency channel reaches clients we cannot reach ourselves, at no per-client acquisition cost |
| Scalability & defensibility | **3** | Best capacity ratio of the four (18–37 clients/FTE) and scalable without headcount per client — but defensibility rests on being cheaper or better-verified than vendor agency tiers, which is fragile |
| **Weighted read** | **Reframe or drop** | As "tooling for agencies" it is dead — vendors own it. As "**verified human delivery + measurement QA that agencies cannot staff**" it survives, and it is the natural fallback if H1 fails. Note the reframe: we would be selling *labour and verification*, not infrastructure |

**Evidence that would invalidate H4:**
1. ≥5 of 7 agencies say they already deliver AI-search work in-house and would not resell or white-label.
2. Agencies will not pay more than vendor agency tiers (€205–€675/mo, $99 + $399/workspace) for anything that
   is not billable delivery labour.
3. Agencies refuse to let a third party touch their client relationships or appear in any client-visible artefact
   (the usual white-label blocker).
4. White-label digital-PR suppliers already cover the delivery layer at $3,000–$10,000/mo retainers, leaving no
   room for a second intermediary.

## Combined read, and what changes in our positioning

| | H1 Managed execution | H2 Vertical specialisation | H3 Outcome intelligence | H4 Agency infrastructure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence quality today | **Best** (documented capacity gap + published price band) | Good (severity documented, universe counted) | **Refuted as a product** | Mixed (channel real, offer must be reframed) |
| Time to first revenue | **Fastest** | Medium | Cannot standalone | Medium |
| Ceiling | $61k–$375k ARR in 24 months bootstrapped | ~$470k ARR for the vertical at 20% penetration | — | Higher per FTE, concentration risk |
| Defensibility | None (reputation only) | **Best available** (network + references) | None | Weak |
| Role in the business | **The offer** | **The wedge** | **The reporting discipline** | **The fallback / scale channel** |

**Recommended combination, unchanged in structure but corrected in language:**
**H1 as the offer, H2 as the wedge, H3 as reporting discipline (never as a product), H4 as the fallback and
scale channel — reframed as verified human delivery and measurement QA, not as tooling.**

**Two corrections this tranche forces on our positioning (both recorded in `../phase-1/09-competitive-threats.md` §2):**

1. **We are not the only ones who publish the statistics.** Peec published Wald-interval and rule-of-three
   methodology with explicit run-count guidance on 29 Sep 2026, computed from ~120M chats. Our claim narrows to
   **reporting practice**: n, intervals, per-surface separation and measured extraction accuracy (F1, κ) inside
   every deliverable, with ≥5 controlled runs per prompt per window — against a category norm of **one run per
   prompt per model per day** and, per an independent audit of 14 platforms, **zero vendors disclosing
   run-to-run variance in their output**. That is a real difference, but it is a practice difference, not a
   knowledge difference, and a vendor could adopt it in one release.
2. **Our collection method is a trade-off, not a superiority.** Peec documents UI scraping of consumer
   interfaces; Profound states it captures from consumer front ends and uses consumer panels for prompt volume.
   Both therefore observe closer to what a real user sees, at scale. We use official APIs plus limited human
   capture because provider terms bar programmatic extraction from consumer products. Our honest claim is
   **compliance, auditability and measured variance** — and we must disclose that our API-based numbers describe
   a related distribution, not the consumer experience (register §7 overlap figures: ~12% ChatGPT/Gemini/
   Copilot top-10 overlap; 28.3% of ChatGPT's most-cited pages have zero Google visibility).

**The single sentence to test in Stage 1** (unchanged from condition 7, now better grounded):
*"Specialized, evidence-backed AI-search optimization delivered as a managed service for B2B companies that lack
sufficient execution capacity."* — **[HYPOTHESIS]** until a buyer demonstrates willingness to pay.
